The fix gets figured out. Then it gets forgotten.

In a lot of shops, the setup tricks and the answers to odd problems live in a few people's heads. Someone works it out, nobody writes it down, and the next time it comes up it gets worked out again. North Bench Labs is working on making it easy to get that down while the work is still in front of you.

What we're trying to find out

Before building more, I want to hear how shops do it now.

I'm talking with people who run, program and operate machines in small shops. I'm not selling anything yet. These are the questions I'm asking:

  1. When someone solves a tricky problem on a machine, where does that answer end up?A binder, a whiteboard, a text thread, one person's memory?
  2. What happens when that person is out, or leaves?How often does the same problem get solved twice?
  3. What gets in the way of writing things down on the floor?Time, dirty hands, no good place to put it, nobody asking for it?
  4. Have you tried anything to fix this, and what happened to it?
  5. If it were easy, what would you want captured first?

Where things stand

Early, small, and tested on a real shop floor.

  • Working day to day in one shop. Notes are dictated and photographed on a phone during the work, then cleaned up into a searchable shop wiki afterward.
  • Building the capture app. Shop Capture is an iPhone app for dictated notes and photos taken one-handed. A TestFlight beta is being set up.
  • Talking with shops. Visiting and calling small machine shops to learn whether this problem looks the same elsewhere, and what a fix would need to be worth using.
